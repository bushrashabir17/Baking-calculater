How the cake calculator works

A "1 lb cake" is the size most bakeries sell: a cake of about one pound after baking, around 15 cm (6 inch) across, serving 6 to 8 people. This calculator uses about 550 g of batter for each pound of cake and scales every ingredient from there.

Every recipe here is written in baker's percentages. That means each ingredient is shown as a percentage of the flour weight, so the balance of flour, fat, sugar, eggs and liquid stays the same at every size. This is how professional bakers scale recipes.

Basic method for all flavors

  1. Heat the oven and grease and line the pan.
  2. Beat the butter and sugar until pale and fluffy, about 3 minutes.
  3. Add the eggs one at a time, beating after each.
  4. Mix the dry ingredients. Add them to the batter in two parts, alternating with the liquid.
  5. Bake, cool for 10 minutes in the pan, then turn out and cool fully before frosting.

Amounts follow standard baking ratios and are approximate. Ovens, pans and ingredients differ, so do a test bake before a big order.

Common questions

How many people does a 1 lb cake serve?

About 6 to 8 slices. A 2 lb cake serves about 12 to 16, a 3 lb cake about 18 to 24, and so on.

Can I use oil instead of butter?

Yes. Use about 75% of the butter weight in oil, for example 83 g of oil in place of 110 g of butter. The cake will be moister but less rich in flavor.

Why use grams instead of cups?

A cup of flour can weigh 110 to 150 g depending on how you scoop it. Grams are exact, so the cake comes out the same every time.

What if my pan is a different size?

Pick the size closest to your pan from the guide. Fill the pan only half full. If the batter is deeper, bake longer at a slightly lower temperature.

What does baker's % mean?

It shows each ingredient compared to the flour. For example, 87% sugar means 87 g of sugar for every 100 g of flour.
